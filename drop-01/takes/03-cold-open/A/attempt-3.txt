COLD OPEN

INT. SELF-STORAGE FACILITY - UNIT 4118 - DAY

A concrete box crammed floor-to-ceiling with forty years of a
man who never threw anything away. Rolled carpets. A canoe.
Nine identical fax machines.

DEIRDRE VOSS, 40s, in gloves, with a label maker clipped to
her belt, consults a clipboard. Her brother GUS, 30s, sits on
a cooler, eating from a bag of chips he found in here.

DEIRDRE
Those are from the nineties.

GUS
They're fine. Salt's a preservative.
That's why Egypt worked.

Deirdre hauls a tarp off a battered green footlocker. A
padlock. On the lid, in Sharpie:

"DO NOT OPEN UNTIL I'M DEAD. I MEAN IT. - DAD"

They both stare at it.

GUS (CONT'D)
Okay, but is he, though? Legally?

DEIRDRE
We buried him Tuesday. You cried at
the buffet.

GUS
I cried at the ham. There's a
difference and you know it.

Deirdre produces bolt cutters from her tote bag. Gus stands.

GUS (CONT'D)
You brought bolt cutters to a
funeral weekend.

DEIRDRE
I brought bolt cutters to Dad.

She cuts the lock. Lifts the lid. Inside: bowling trophies,
a tangle of extension cords, and — nestled in a folded
sweater — a brass URN.

Gus leans in. Reads the engraving.

GUS
"Barbara Ann Voss. Beloved Wife.
1954 to 1998."

Long pause. Deirdre sets down the bolt cutters.

DEIRDRE
Mom's in the Corolla.

GUS
Mom's in the Corolla eating a
Frosty.

DEIRDRE
So who is this?

GUS
Don't shake it!

DEIRDRE
I'm not shaking it, I'm assessing
it —

A shadow in the doorway. BARBARA VOSS, 71, very much alive,
Frosty in hand, sunglasses pushed up on her head. She sees
the urn. Stops chewing. Not alarmed. Almost tender.

BARBARA
Oh, good. You found me.

She turns and walks back toward the car. Beat.

GUS
I'm gonna need you to unpack that.

BARBARA
(calling back, mouth full)
Not on an empty stomach, sweetheart!

Deirdre and Gus look at the urn. At each other. Gus offers
her the chips. She takes one.
