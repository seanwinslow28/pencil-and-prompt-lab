COLD OPEN

EXT. WILLOW GLEN MEMORIAL PARK - MORNING

Fog burning off rolling green lawns. Headstones in tidy
rows. A sprinkler ticks.

MARGO OKONKWO (40s, blazer over a fleece vest, clipboard,
the relentless cheer of a woman on commission) leads DENNIS
PULASKI (70s, velour tracksuit, brand-new hiking poles) up a
gentle slope.

                         MARGO
          Now, this is one of our most requested
          sections. Eastern exposure, mature oak,
          very low foot traffic.

Dennis stops. Plants a pole. Squints at the horizon like a
man buying a timeshare.

                         DENNIS
          I can hear the freeway.

                         MARGO
          That's the creek.

                         DENNIS
          It's the freeway.

                         MARGO
          It's the creek, Dennis.

                         DENNIS
               (grudging)
          It's a nice creek.

They walk on. Dennis produces a tape measure and, without
breaking stride, measures a plot.

                         MARGO
          They're all the same size.

                         DENNIS
          That's what they told my brother-in-law.
          He's in a drawer in Reseda like a
          hanging file.

                         MARGO
               (consulting clipboard)
          This one's available. Lovely spot. You'd
          be right beside the Hendersons.

                         DENNIS
          Jim Henderson?

                         MARGO
          Ruth Henderson.

                         DENNIS
          Is Jim coming?

                         MARGO
          Eventually. That's sort of the business
          model.

                         DENNIS
          Then no. That man's dog did his business
          on my hydrangeas for eleven years and he
          never once acknowledged it. I'm not
          spending eternity next to a man not
          apologizing.

They crest the hill. Below: a small pond, willows, ducks.
A postcard.

                         DENNIS
               (quiet)
          Now that. That's the one.

                         MARGO
               (hesitant)
          I'd steer you off the pond.

                         DENNIS
          Why? It's beautiful.

                         MARGO
          It floods. Every four, five years we get
          a wet spring, water table comes up,
          and... the residents shift.

                         DENNIS
          Shift.

                         MARGO
          Migrate. Slightly. Toward the parking
          lot.

Dennis looks out over the water a long moment. Something
moves behind his eyes. He plants both poles like a flag.

                         DENNIS
          Marguerite. I have never once left this
          county.

                         MARGO
          Dennis --

                         DENNIS
          Put me by the water.
